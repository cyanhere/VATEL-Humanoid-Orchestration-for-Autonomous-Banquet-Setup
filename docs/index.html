VATEL

Humanoid Orchestration for Autonomous Banquet Setup

CDE4301 Innovation & Design Capstone · College of Design and Engineering, National University of Singapore · AY2026/27

The problem

Banquet table setting requires handling diverse, fragile tableware across an environment purpose-built for human reach and access, where fixed automation is impractical and a separate task-specific robot per item type is uneconomical. VATEL develops and evaluates an autonomous perception-and-manipulation pipeline for a humanoid robot to transfer and place banquet tableware. The representative case is plate transfer from an AGV, a requirement from our industry partner, who is deploying the AGV. The approach is intended to generalise to other tableware handling tasks.

How it works

  1. Plan the banquet. The banquet team opens the VATEL Planner on an iPad or a computer. It shows the whole banquet hall, segments out the tables and draws them on a 2-D map, where the team plans the arrangement and setup for the event.
  2. Hand the plan to the robot. When the arrangement is done, the planner sends the static map to the Unitree R1 humanoid: where each table is, how many sets of plates and cutlery each table needs, and the calculated position of every set.
  3. Map the real hall and navigate. The R1 navigates the hall autonomously from the planner's static map. Its onboard Hesai JT128 lidar maps the real room to find the exact location of each table, correcting the difference between the plan and the real arrangement. The robot updates the map and navigates to each set position.
  4. Transfer plates from the AGV. Once the robot can navigate the whole hall and reach at least 80 % of the required workspace, it goes to the AGV plate-delivery area, where an AGV brings plates stacked from the kitchen. It takes one plate at a time off the stack and places it at its designated position. One plate per trip is a deliberate limit, set by the dexterous hands and the robot platform.
  5. Done. The task is complete when the plates are at their designated positions, with up to 10 % of all plates allowed to miss, and no plate broken.

HandsBrainCo Revo 2 dexterous hands
Onboard computeNVIDIA Jetson Orin
Plate deliveryAGV with stacked plates, deployed by our industry partner
PlannerAny web browser, on an iPad or a computer
